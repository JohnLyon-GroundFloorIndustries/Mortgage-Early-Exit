The Early-Exit Mortgage Trap: Note Rate vs. Equivalent Equity APR

Your lender charges the note rate (e.g., 5.00% APR) on whatever balance you still owe, every month, whether you sell in Year 1 or Year 30. What an early exit changes is how little of your cash has become equity: the balance is largest in the early years, so most of each early payment is interest. The Equivalent Equity APR below is a Ground Floor illustration of that gap. It prices the equity you keep as if every payment you made had gone toward buying it. It is not your borrowing cost.

How the "Equivalent Equity APR" Is Calculated (Illustrative Metric)

After m monthly payments of $2,147.29, you have built Em in principal equity and paid Im in interest. We treat Em as if it were a standalone loan repaid by those same payments and solve, by bisection, for the monthly rate r where Σ PMTt ÷ (1 + r)t = Em. It is shown two ways: APR = r × 12, the same convention as your note rate, and effective annual = (1 + r)12 − 1. At full term the result equals your note rate exactly, which is the tie-out check. The figure describes how little equity early payments buy. Your actual borrowing cost stays at the note rate.

Ground Floor Advisory Playbook

Strategic Playbook for Short-Term Holders (3–7 Year Horizons)

If you know your holding period is 3 to 7 years, deploy these four structural strategies to bypass front-loaded amortization drag.

PILLAR 01 15-Yr Fixed

Match Term to Timeline

A 15-year fixed builds principal roughly 3x faster from the first payment and usually carries a lower rate (6.45% vs. 7.11% on average, Freddie Mac, Nov 12, 2026). The trade-off is a payment about +47% higher on this loan at the same rate.

Interactive Preset:
PILLAR 02 5/6 or 7/6 ARM

Align Rate Lock to Stay

A 5/6 or 7/6 adjustable-rate mortgage holds a lower fixed rate for the first five or seven years, then resets every six months. Recent quotes have run roughly 0.4 to 1.0 point under the 30-year fixed. The risk: if plans change and you stay past the reset, your rate and payment can rise.

Rate Spread Test:
PILLAR 03 2–4 Unit Multifamily

Small Multifamily / House Hack

Buy a duplex, triplex or fourplex and live in one unit (or invest in commercial multifamily) so tenant rent helps carry the interest. Rental income is not guaranteed: vacancy, repairs and management are real costs, and private offerings carry their own risks and investor-eligibility rules.

Debt Service Coverage: Tenant-Amortized Equity
PILLAR 04 Liquidity vs. Friction

Rent & Deploy Liquid Capital

Buying typically costs 2%–5% of the loan and selling 6%–10% of the price. Over a short hold those costs can outweigh the equity you build, so renting and investing the difference can come out ahead. Run your own numbers in the All-In Reality Check before deciding.
